The Missing Access Control

Task 1 Briefing ▾

It is 9:00 AM at TechCorp.

Alex, a newly joined Junior Cybersecurity Analyst, receives his first assignment from Sarah, the Security Team Lead.

Sarah: "Alex, we have a problem with our Employee Management Portal. A few employees reported that they could see pages that should belong to managers and administrators. I want you to investigate it."

TechCorp recently launched an internal Employee Management Portal. The portal has three roles:

  • 👤 Employee
  • 👨‍💼 Manager
  • 👑 Administrator

Each role should have access only to the resources required for that role. Alex is given a normal Employee training account for the investigation.

Alex logs into the portal successfully. At first, everything looks normal. He can access the Dashboard and My Profile. But Alex remembers Sarah's warning:

Sarah: "Logging in proves who you are. It does not automatically prove what you are allowed to access."

Alex decides not to assume the portal is secure. His task is to investigate how the application handles access control.

Task 2 Mission 1 — Identify Your Identity ▾

Alex starts with the Employee account provided by Sarah.

username: alex
password: Alex@123

Your task

  1. Log in using the Employee training account.
  2. Confirm that the login succeeds.
  3. Identify which role the application has assigned to your account.
  4. Record any interesting information shown after login.

Question to think about: what security process has successfully verified Alex's identity? Don't look for the definition yet — use the application's behavior to work it out.

Task 3 Mission 2 — Map the Portal ▾

Alex explores the application to understand what an Employee can normally access. He discovers:

Portal AreaExpected Employee Access
Dashboard✅ Allowed
My Profile✅ Allowed
Team Management❌ Restricted
Admin Panel❌ Restricted

However, Sarah specifically asked Alex to investigate whether the restrictions are actually enforced by the server.

Your task — explore the available pages and observe:

  • What happens when you access an allowed page?
  • What happens when you access a restricted page?
  • Does the browser reveal anything useful?
  • Are the requests or responses different between allowed and restricted resources?
Task 4 Mission 3 — Follow the Evidence ▾

Alex notices that the application is making requests to different portal resources. Instead of simply accepting the Access Denied message, he decides to inspect the application's behavior.

Your task — use your browser's developer tools or Burp Suite to investigate the requests. Look for clues such as:

  • URLs/endpoints
  • Parameters
  • Cookies/session information
  • Role-related information
  • Server responses

Goal — determine where the application appears to make its access-control decision.

💡 Don't guess the answer. Follow the evidence from the application's requests and responses.

Look at the requests made when you navigate between portal pages. What cookies does your browser send along with every request?
Compare a request for a page you can access (like /dashboard) with one you cannot access (like /admin). Are there any values in the cookies that describe who you are or what you're allowed to do?
Ask yourself — is the server making this authorization decision itself by checking a trusted, tamper-proof source (like a database lookup tied to your login session), or is it trusting a piece of information that came from the client and could be edited before the request even reaches the server?
Task 5 Mission 4 — The Broken Door ▾

Alex discovers that the portal's authentication mechanism is working, but something about authorization deserves closer investigation. The Employee account is legitimate. The session is valid. Yet Alex finds an interesting difference between being logged in and being allowed to access a resource.

Your task — investigate whether the Employee account can reach a restricted resource that should belong to a higher-privileged role. Use only the provided lab environment.

Success condition — find the protected resource and determine why the application should have denied the Employee access.

Task 6 Mission 5 — Explain the Incident ▾

Alex reports his findings to Sarah.

Alex: "The login system correctly identified me as an Employee. The problem is that authentication and authorization are separate security decisions."

Sarah asks Alex to prove that he understands what happened.

Answer the following (for your own notes):

  1. Who was Alex authenticated as?
  2. What resource was Alex not supposed to access?
  3. Was Alex authenticated successfully?
  4. Was Alex authorized to access the restricted resource?
  5. Which security control was responsible for deciding whether he could access it?
  6. What security weakness allowed the unexpected access?
Task 7 🏁 Final Challenge ▾

Complete the investigation with a short knowledge check.

🎓 What Alex Learned

🔐 Authentication — "Who are you?"
It establishes the identity of the user.

🛡️ Authorization — "What are you allowed to access or do?"
It determines whether the authenticated user has permission to access a resource.

⭐ Key Lesson: Successfully logging into a system does not mean you are authorized to access everything inside it.
